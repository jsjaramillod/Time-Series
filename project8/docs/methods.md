# Statistical methods and interpretation

## Common factors

Each standardized series is a loading times a shared AR(1) factor plus independent Gaussian noise. The innovation variance of the factor is fixed at one. Loadings have independent standard normal priors, and each measurement precision has a Gamma(1, 1) prior in shape–rate form. Persistence has a standard normal prior restricted to the stationary interval. Sales anchors the sign for monthly activity and SPY anchors the daily market factor. When necessary, the entire factor path and all loadings are reflected together.

The Gibbs sampler draws the complete latent path from its joint conditional distribution by forward filtering and backward sampling. Bidiagonal triangular solves evaluate the scalar recursions. Tests compare draws with direct conditioning on a small joint Gaussian system. Loadings, measurement variances and persistence are then updated. The 400-draw coursework replay uses the original seed, 200 warmup iterations and the original truncated-normal persistence update. The portfolio fits also account for the density of the stationary initial state when drawing persistence. The [column-only comparison](../results/column_audit.csv) keeps the original seed and sampler unchanged while reallocating the eight observations.

The equity sampler adds a joint scale update for the latent path and loadings. A standard deviation of 1.4 divided by the square root of sample size defines the symmetric normal proposal for log scale. Multiplying the path by this scale and dividing the loadings by it leaves the observation means unchanged. The acceptance ratio includes the stationary path density, Gaussian loading priors and the change-of-variables factor. This update targets the same posterior and improves mixing when the funds co-move closely. Four independent chains are checked using split R-hat and bulk and tail effective sample sizes for persistence, all loadings and all measurement variances. Intervals for the paths and parameters are marginal posterior percentile intervals. A factor estimated using all dates describes contemporaneous history. [Carter and Kohn](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/81.3.541)

## Forecast evaluation

For each forecast of Y at t+1 the slopes use Y at t through t−3, plus either the 100 predictors at t or principal components of those predictors. With information through observation t, the regression has response rows 5 through t. PCA centers the raw predictor panel using only rows 1 through t and estimates the loading vectors anew. Ridge centers the training design, divides columns by their training sample standard deviations and minimizes the sum of squared residuals plus the penalty times the sum of squared slopes. The intercept is unpenalized. The raw-unit course reconstruction omits division by the standard deviations. OLS needs no regularization.

A separate validation sample chooses the specification using only targets 201–300. Each validation prediction is itself fitted on earlier observations. Coefficients and preprocessing are updated through each new origin during the final evaluation, while the chosen specification stays fixed. The scoring file reports root mean squared error, mean absolute error and signed bias. Bootstrap comparisons resample contiguous blocks of paired loss observations. The interval describes this panel and does not encompass changing the identity of the target or adopting a different validation period.

## Technology and hours

The bivariate growth VAR uses four lags of the natural log changes in output per hour and total hours. Coefficient means are zero. At lag k the prior standard deviation of coefficient (i,j) is tightness times the ratio of the two OLS residual standard deviations divided by k. The intercept variance is ten times its equation's residual variance. The covariance has an inverse-Wishart prior with four degrees of freedom and scale equal to the diagonal of the residual variances. The source uses hyperparameters 0.5, 1 and 10; the submission does not specify all covariance and scale conventions. This is an independently specified refit. The sensitivity halves the lag-coefficient standard deviations. Each sample calibrates residual scales on its own training data.

The reduced-form parameters are drawn from Gaussian and inverse-Wishart conditionals. Each of four chains retains 1,200 draws after 400 warmup iterations. Given a stable draw, form the long-run reduced-form multiplier, factor its implied long-run covariance using lower-triangular Cholesky, and solve back for the impact matrix. The nontechnology shock has zero permanent effect on productivity, and a positive technology shock raises long-run productivity. We check both the restriction and the implied reduced-form covariance on each retained draw. Stability requires all companion roots inside the unit circle, and the condition-number ceiling for the long-run solve is 100 million. The public diagnostics show how many draws were eligible.

Horizon one is the impact quarter. The sum of the responses of log growth gives the response of log hours to a one-standard-deviation structural shock. Multiplying by 100 gives log percentage points. Wide posterior intervals and dependence on the identifying restriction limit the economic interpretation. [Galí (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.89.1.249)

The [archived coefficient table](../data/coursework_var.csv) and [refitted posterior means](../results/var_coefficients.csv) provide the complete reduced-form comparison for the two equations, including the intercepts. In the original window the previous quarter’s hours-growth coefficient in the hours equation was 0.601 in class and 0.595 in the refit. The fourth productivity lag in that equation changed from +0.026 to −0.008. The impact probability thus agrees more closely than every individual coefficient.
